Section 07 · My capstone journey

Scope, adaptation, activity, and learning

How the project evolved

The capstone began with a broad concern about Black student flourishing and the structural conditions affecting Graduation Coaching. It became stronger as the scope was contained without containing the justice claim — moving from trying to explain every dimension of anti-Black racism in education toward a disciplined contribution focused on role clarity, institutional readiness, pathway protection, coach sustainability, and accountability.

Mentor transition

Rheba Moore-Nash supported the early direction of the work before going on leave. The transition to Colette Greaves required restating the purpose, clarifying the scope, and taking greater ownership of the project's through-line. That transition was not simply an administrative change — it became a practical lesson in continuity, adaptive leadership, communication, and the importance of making project logic visible enough that another person can enter the work without losing its integrity.

The project became stronger when it became more contained. Scope was not a retreat from justice; it was the condition that made the work usable. Reflective learning

Key turning points

  • Reframing the issue from student support alone to institutional readiness.
  • Separating relational care from the institutional obligation to change systems.
  • Distinguishing historical and provincial evidence from local TVDSB findings.
  • Defining the framework as a testable prototype rather than a validated model.
  • Treating coach sustainability and racial labour as implementation conditions, not personal resilience issues.
  • Using the Capstone Conference to translate a complex argument into a concise public narrative.
