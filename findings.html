Section 04 · What the work revealed

Six findings from the synthesis

FindingWhat it demonstrates
1. Graduation is necessary but insufficientA graduation rate cannot reveal whether students experienced belonging, equitable course access, strong credit accumulation, meaningful choice, or a pathway aligned with their aspirations.
2. Belonging is an educational conditionIdentity safety, recognition, trust, and connection affect whether students participate, disclose concerns, seek support, persist, and imagine a future within the institution.
3. Relationships are infrastructureTrusted relationships are not peripheral to implementation. They are the mechanism through which needs, strengths, barriers, and institutional patterns become visible.
4. Coaches protect pathway qualityThe role extends through course selection, credits, transitions, advocacy, postsecondary planning, and preservation of future choice.
5. Families and communities are knowledge holdersFamilies and Black communities are not audiences for institutional decisions. Their interpretation, history, and knowledge are necessary to define problems and legitimate solutions.
6. Responsibility cannot be transferred to Black staffGraduation Coaches can interrupt harm and generate insight, but the duty to dismantle anti-Black racism belongs to the institution. The role requires authority, resources, boundaries, leadership response, and protection from disproportionate racial and emotional labour.
The strongest pattern is that coaches are most powerful when relationships with students create institutional learning, not only individual intervention. Central synthesis finding

The institutional risk

Without a defined pathway from relational knowledge to leadership action, the coach role can become compensatory: a caring person helps students navigate around barriers while the policies, practices, expectations, and decision structures producing those barriers remain intact. That model can improve individual experience while shielding the institution from learning.

The institutional opportunity

When coach knowledge is de-identified, aggregated, documented, and connected to clear escalation and response structures, the role becomes part of the institution's learning and accountability architecture. Relational practice and systems change are then mutually reinforcing rather than competing responsibilities.
