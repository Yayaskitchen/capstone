Section 05 · The Prepared for Black Students framework

Five domains, seven functions, one feedback loop

A five-domain readiness framework

The framework connects the student-facing and system-facing dimensions of Graduation Coaching. It begins with flourishing, not remediation, and it places accountability and sustainability around the role so that care does not become an excuse for institutional inaction.

Belonging, agency, dignity, identity affirmation, learning, well-being, achievement, leadership, joy, and future possibility.

Trust, presence, culturally responsive navigation, advocacy, continuity, and support grounded in the whole student.

Reciprocal engagement, community knowledge, transparent decision pathways, cultural legitimacy, and shared interpretation.

Role integration, pathway protection, leadership response, policy and practice change, resource decisions, and improvement planning.

Mandate, authority, workload, well-being, data, evaluation, transparency, funding, continuity, and responsibility beyond individual champions.

Learning + response feedback loop — each domain feeds the next, and institutional response feeds back into student flourishing.

Seven coach functions

The functions are integrated rather than sequential. Their purpose is to protect against reducing the role to credit recovery, crisis response, cultural representation, or informal problem solving.

Trusted relational anchor

Build continuity, safety, trust, and a relationship through which students can be fully seen and heard.

Culturally responsive navigator

Interpret and navigate school structures without asking students to abandon identity or accommodate injustice.

Pathway guardian

Protect course access, credit accumulation, transitions, postsecondary options, and future choice.

Student voice amplifier

Create safe routes for student priorities and interpretations to influence decisions.

Barrier interrupter

Act on immediate inequities while documenting patterns that require wider action.

Institutional witness

Make recurring experiences and institutional responses visible without exposing individual students.

Transformation catalyst

Connect situated knowledge to leadership response, improvement planning, policy, resources, and evaluation.

Theory of change

Relationship

Trusted proximity makes concerns, strengths, and barriers visible.

System intelligence

Recurring patterns are documented without exposing students.

Leadership response

Clear escalation, ownership, resources, and timelines follow.

Institutional change

Policy, practice, pathways, and accountability are altered.

The core test

The coach should not be evaluated only by the number of students served. The system must also be evaluated by whether recurring barriers are identified, owned, acted upon, and prevented from recurring.
