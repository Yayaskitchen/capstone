Section 06 · From insight to action

Eight recommendations and an evaluation agenda

Implementation priorities

PriorityImplementation mechanismEvidence of progress
1. Role charterDefine mandate, authority, boundaries, escalation, decision rights, and protected functions.Approved charter; staff understanding; fewer role conflicts; documented escalation routes.
2. Core integrationConnect coaches to Student Success, guidance, school improvement, transition planning, and leadership structures.Regular participation; defined referral routes; coach insight reflected in plans and decisions.
3. Pathway monitoringMonitor course access, credits, streaming, transitions, identification, graduation, and postsecondary choice.Disaggregated pathway indicators; identified gaps; documented corrective action.
4. Community voiceCreate decision pathways for students, families, and Black communities — not only consultation events.Participation at decision points; response records; community interpretation influences action.
5. Black-affirming spacesResource Black joy, affinity, curriculum, leadership, history, and identity-affirming learning.Student-defined belonging; participation; continuity; resourcing beyond short-term events.
6. Feedback loopMove de-identified recurring patterns from coach practice to named leadership ownership and response.Response time; action owner; status tracking; recurrence; closure communicated back.
7. Coach sustainabilityProtect capacity, well-being, authority, role boundaries, professional learning, and continuity.Manageable workload; retention; supervision; reduced role overload; protected development time.
8. Evaluate changeAssess flourishing, pathway quality, family trust, coach sustainability, and institutional response.Baseline and follow-up measures; mixed methods; transparent reporting; documented system change.

Evaluation must measure the institution

A serious evaluation should include student outcomes, but it should not place the full burden of proof on students. The institution must also show whether it listened, responded, changed, resourced the work, protected the role, and reduced the recurrence of identified barriers.

Evidence dimensionWhat it demonstrates
Student flourishingBelonging, agency, identity affirmation, safety, well-being, achievement, leadership, and joy.
Pathway qualityCourse access, credits, transitions, graduation, identification, postsecondary options, and preserved choice.
Family and community trustAccess, responsiveness, influence over decisions, transparency, and confidence in institutional follow-through.
Coach sustainabilityRole clarity, authority, workload, retention, supervision, well-being, professional learning, and continuity.
Institutional responseResponse time, decision ownership, resource allocation, policy or practice change, recurrence, and communication of closure.
The goal is not to help Black students endure inequitable systems with more resilience. The goal is to build systems where that extraordinary resilience is no longer required for them to belong, achieve, and flourish. Capstone conclusion

Phased implementation logic

Immediate
Confirm mandate, role boundaries, escalation, protected time, and leadership ownership. Minimum evidence: approved role charter; named decision owners; communication to schools and communities.
Development
Co-design tools, establish baseline measures, and align pathways, community voice, and improvement planning. Minimum evidence: co-development record; baseline; prototype revisions; data and governance agreements.
Pilot & learn
Implement a bounded pilot and assess student experience, pathway quality, coach sustainability, and system response. Minimum evidence: mixed-method findings; response-time record; recurrence analysis; public learning report.
